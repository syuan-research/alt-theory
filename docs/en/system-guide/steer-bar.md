# The Steer Bar

Near the composer, beside the role and knowledge controls, the Steer bar
holds a short set of Alt Theory's bundled skills for the moments a
particular way of working applies. It is the on-demand way to press a
method into the conversation.

## What Steer is for

Steer is for situational methods: a way of working that fits the next few
turns but is not the whole conversation. Pressing a button sends the
skill as a framing that rides your next message. The alignment interview,
a clarification reset, or a guided next step each belong here when the
moment calls for them.

The skills offered on the Steer bar are the bundled skills written for
this purpose. The bar is not the full skill catalog; every skill remains
reachable through the [command palette](commands.md) and the
[Toolbox](toolbox.md).

## Using a button

- Press a button to steer the next few turns with that skill. The framing
  rides your next message; it does not spend the message on its own.
- Press again or pick another button to switch.
- Double-click to lock a way of working in place until you release it.
  A locked skill stays active past the few-turn window; release sends one
  line saying it is off.

Each button's tooltip names the skill and what it does. A name that does
not fit shows in full on hover.

## Card slot

The composer's top area is a switchable card slot: the role and knowledge
controls are one card, Steer is another. Switching between them shows one
line on what each is for. This is why Steer replaces the role and
knowledge controls while open rather than sitting beside them.

## Which skills appear

The bundled skills written for steering:

- adaptive-aligning is the alignment interview before direction-setting
  work ([catalog](bundled-skills.md#adaptive-aligning)).
- confirm-why confirms the understood reason behind a request, then
  offers options based on that reason.
- guided-next-steps gives the next step after alignment and explains how
  it serves the goal, with later options.
- clear-misunderstanding restarts communication in a few low-friction
  turns when something feels stuck or misread.

adaptive-aligning is the strongest of these: the moment it is activated
or mentioned, the agent stops and discusses before doing any work. Some
faster models used to skip straight to executing; the skill now insists.

## Relation to Understand and Work

Steer is available in both modes. A skill whose method needs tools
remains Work-only by its own text, as on the rest of the skill system
([how skills work](how-skills-work.md)).
