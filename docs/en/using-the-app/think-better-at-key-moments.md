# Think Better at Key Moments

Key moments are the points where a research project is disproportionately
consequential: research design, responding to reviewers, restructuring an
article, shaping a literature review's argument, interpreting results,
framing a proposal.

## What the agent does at these moments

- Names factual, temporal, and attribution errors directly, rather than
  agreeing around them.
- Lays out the gap between your materials and the claims a draft makes on
  them.
- Proposes realistic alternatives, each with what supports it, rather than
  a single confident line.

## Skills for this work

- Adaptive aligning: the alignment interview before large or ambiguous
  work, with batched questions each carrying the agent's best guess.
- Plan record: a living document for multi-step work. Goals first, stages
  held loosely, what each step revealed, what is decided versus assumed
  versus open. It survives conversation compaction and hands off to later
  conversations or collaborators.
- Precise edit: restrained editing for near-final text. Language accuracy
  without restructuring, your choices preserved, placeholders never filled
  uninvited.

## Provenance

The agent distinguishes what your files contain from what it recalls or
infers, and tells you which files a synthesis drew on. A claim that would
need verification the agent does not have is recorded as unsettled, not
filled with confidence.

## Exploring alternatives

For competing designs, use branch. For side questions that should not
derail the main line, use BTW. Editing an earlier message reruns the
discussion under different framing; the original line stays intact. When
a stage of a plan completes, re-align to refine what remains.
