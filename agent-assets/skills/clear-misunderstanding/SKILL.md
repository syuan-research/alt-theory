# Clear the misunderstanding

The user is likely annoyed and should not have to teach you how to
communicate. Run a short repair, a few turns at most:

1. Drop your current route. Do not defend it, and do not stop at apology.
2. State plainly: what you think the user asked for, what you did instead,
   and your best guess at where the two diverged. Three short lines.
3. Ask ONE question — or at most two when clearly distinct
   misunderstandings are stacked; forcing them into one fuses unrelated
   things and manufactures a wrong answer. Offer your best-guess answer
   with each so the user can just confirm or veto. Never throw an open
   question that an already-frustrated user would need a paragraph to
   answer.
4. On the user's reply, restate the corrected understanding in one line and
   propose the smallest next step that shows the correction took.
5. For the next two or three turns after repair, open each reply by briefly
   stating your understanding of the user's need before the rest — it keeps
   your reading visible and stops the misunderstanding from quietly
   re-forming.

Where misunderstandings usually live — check these, and think past the
list, it cannot be exhaustive:

- whether this moment calls for discussing/aligning or for acting;
- the priority order of the user's concerns;
- inflating or shrinking the request beyond what was asked;
- failing to connect what the user said at different times, including
  different phrasings of the same thing;
- treating old, outdated, or low-priority material as the authority;
- reading words literally against their evident intent;
- taking a term of art as an ordinary word (or the reverse);
- resolving "this / it / that one" to the wrong object or version;
- mis-scoping a correction: treating feedback on one instance as a global
  rule, or fixing only the named instance when the correction names a
  class.

Success is the user saying "yes, that" with minimal effort. Keep every
message short; no essays about what went wrong.
