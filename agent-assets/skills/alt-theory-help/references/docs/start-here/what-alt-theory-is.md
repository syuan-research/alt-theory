# What Alt Theory Is

Alt Theory is a research workspace for the social sciences. At its center
is a conversation with an AI collaborator that takes thinking seriously:
it distinguishes what is known from what is inferred, marks uncertainty
instead of papering over it, and would rather tell you it cannot verify
something than invent a citation. When your work moves from thinking to
doing — searching literature, reading and producing documents, working
through the files of a real project — the same conversation can take on
that work too.

## Careful thinking is the baseline

Most AI tools are tuned to produce a confident, polished answer quickly.
For research, that speed has a cost: a plausible answer can hide the
conceptual jumps, the missing evidence, and the alternatives you did not
consider. Alt Theory is built the other way around. Its default behavior
is to:

- surface assumptions and gaps between your framing and the evidence,
  rather than smoothing them over;
- separate what it found, what it remembers but has not verified, and what
  it inferred — and label each;
- offer realistic options when something is genuinely open, instead of
  false certainty or false binaries;
- move in moderate steps, so you can steer, rather than delivering a
  finished framework you did not choose.

This is not caution for its own sake. It is what makes the tool usable for
consequential work — a research design, a response to reviewers, a grant
proposal — where being agreeably wrong is worse than being slower.

## Thinking and doing, in one conversation

Every Alt Theory conversation runs in one of two modes, and you can switch
between them as the work changes:

- **Understand** is for careful dialogue with bounded reach. The agent
  works with what you bring into the conversation and what it knows,
  labelled honestly.
- **Work** opens practical capabilities: working folders on your computer,
  live web and literature search, document reading and production, and
  agent actions that you approve as they cross boundaries.

Both are agentic; the difference is how much of your computer and tool
ecosystem you have chosen to open to the agent.
[Understand and Work](understand-and-work.md) explains the model.

## Methods you can read

Much of Alt Theory's behavior is carried by **bundled skills** — readable
instruction files that encode how it searches with provenance, how it
aligns with you before large or ambiguous work, how it keeps plan records
that survive long conversations, how it edits near-final text with
restraint, and how it organizes files it creates. They are not hidden
tuning: you can open and read every one, and your own skills can take
precedence over them. See [Bundled Skills](../system-guide/bundled-skills.md).

## Who it is for

Alt Theory is built for students and researchers in the social sciences —
from a master's student framing a first research question to an
experienced researcher deciding whether a tool belongs anywhere near a
grant proposal. It assumes no prior experience with AI agent tools. If you
do run agent harnesses already, the product is built on the Pi ecosystem
and plays well with it; see
[The App and Plugins](app-and-plugins.md).

## Where the product stands

Alt Theory is in active alpha development. The core app is usable and in
real research use; interfaces and configuration may still change, and the
[known limitations](../help/compatibility-formats-limitations.md) page is
kept honest. Alt Theory is also a long-term research project about what
kind of AI support scholars actually need; if that research interests you,
see [Releases and Further Help](../help/releases-and-further-help.md) for
how to get in touch.
