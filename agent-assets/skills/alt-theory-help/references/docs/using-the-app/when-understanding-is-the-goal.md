# When Understanding Is the Goal

Sometimes understanding is not preparation for the "real" work — it is the
work. Exploring a direction you might commit a year to. Working out what
two nearby concepts actually distinguish. Examining a theory's assumptions
before building on it. Developing a view that does not belong to any
single project yet.

A note before the scenarios: this page is about a *goal*, not a mode. Most
understanding-centered work fits naturally in Understand mode, but you can
pursue it in Work mode too — for instance when it needs live literature
search. The three pages in this section describe kinds of work; the
[mode choice](../start-here/understand-and-work.md) stays yours throughout.

## What this work looks like here

Bring the unfinished thing as it is. A half-formed question, pages of
contradictory notes, a concept you suspect is doing too much work. The
agent's job is not to resolve it quickly; it is to help you see it
clearly:

- **Your framing gets examined, not just extended.** If your question
  mixes place attachment with habit, the response distinguishes them and
  shows what each framing commits you to — rather than picking one
  silently and building on it.
- **Gaps stay visible.** Where your idea and the evidence do not fully
  fit, the mismatch is laid out. The agent will not trim a theory's edges
  to make it fit your case.
- **Uncertainty is labelled, not filled.** Claims come marked: established
  and stable, plausible but unverified, or genuinely open. Where
  verification would need live sources, the agent says exactly that.
- **Movement is stepwise.** You get the next useful distinction or
  question, with realistic alternatives where things are open — not a
  complete framework you did not choose.

## Shaping the conversation

Two levers matter most for understanding-centered work:

- **Knowledge bases** put curated domain material within the agent's
  reach — enable one when your discussion should be grounded in it, and
  ask the agent to connect your idea to specific sources in it.
- **Roles** change presentation — more Socratic or more direct, more
  tutoring or more critical. No role changes the principles; a gentler
  voice does not become a more agreeable one.

Long-running understanding is also worth preserving deliberately. Ask for
a [conversation summary](../system-guide/bundled-skills.md) when a
discussion reaches a resting point, or keep the developing picture in a
file so it outlives any single conversation.

## Verification has a place in the flow

Understanding-work constantly brushes against checkable facts — does that
study actually say that? is this the current state of the literature? In
Understand mode, the agent handles these honestly without live lookup:
what it recalls is marked unverified (for citations, explicitly so), and
what it cannot support it declines to invent.

When verification becomes the actual next step, switch the same
conversation to Work: academic search returns real, DOI-verified
references with citation counts, and pages can be fetched and quoted with
sources. The discussion picks up where it was, now on checked ground.
[Search, Sources, and Web Content](../system-guide/search-sources-web.md)
explains the discipline behind this.

## If you are relying on AI more than you would like

Students often depend heavily on AI advice while knowing its central risk:
it is convincing whether or not it is right, and there is not always a
supervisor with time to check. Alt Theory cannot replace supervision, but
it is built to be examinable in a way general chat tools are not:

- Ask it to separate what supports each claim — found, recalled, or
  inferred — and it will.
- Ask "what would make this framing wrong?" and it treats that as a real
  question, not an invitation to reassure you.
- Its default is to expose the weakest link in an argument rather than
  polish over it — including in its own suggestions.

Used this way, the tool becomes practice in the judgment it might
otherwise erode.
