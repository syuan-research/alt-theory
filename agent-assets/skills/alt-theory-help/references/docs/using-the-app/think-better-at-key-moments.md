# Think Better at Key Moments in Your Research Work

Some moments in a research project are disproportionately consequential:
settling a research design, responding to reviewers, restructuring an
article, shaping a literature review's argument, deciding what a result
means for the larger narrative, framing a proposal. Production is not the
bottleneck at these moments — judgment is.

(As throughout this section: this is a kind of work, not a mode. These
moments often live in Understand, and reach for Work when materials or
verification come into play.)

## Why a careful tool matters exactly here

At a key moment, an agreeable assistant is a liability. If you show a
reviewer response to a tool that praises it, you learn nothing; if you ask
for a research design and get a confident template, the weak joint is now
hidden inside something that looks finished. Alt Theory's value at these
moments is that it pushes back where the evidence pushes back:

- factual, temporal, and attribution errors get named directly, even when
  your overall direction is right;
- the gap between what your materials support and what your draft claims
  is laid out, not smoothed;
- when it proposes options, they are realistic alternatives with what
  supports each — so the decision stays visibly yours.

## The methods that carry these moments

Three bundled skills exist mainly for this kind of work. You can invoke
them from the Toolbox, by name, or simply describe what you want:

- **Aligning** — before a large or ambiguous piece of work, the agent
  interviews you: batched questions, each with its own best guesses
  attached, so you can see how far apart you are and correct the framing
  before anything is built on it. Say "align with me first" — or it will
  propose alignment itself when acting on guesses would be expensive.
- **Plan records** — for work that spans conversations and days, a living document that
  plans forward and records backward: goals, stages, what each step
  revealed, what changed. Decisions written here survive conversation
  compaction and are readable by your future self, a collaborator, or a
  later conversation.
- **Precise edit** — for near-final text, restrained language work that
  respects your structure, voice, and deliberate choices, flagging only
  what you do not already know.

## Exploring alternatives without losing the line

Key moments usually involve alternatives worth taking seriously.
[Branch](../system-guide/responses-and-controls.md) the conversation to
develop the competing design; use **BTW** for a side question that should
not derail the main discussion; revise an earlier message to see how the
discussion runs under different framing. Nothing is lost: the original
line stays intact while you look around it.

## Materials that are incomplete, contested, or moving

Real key-moment materials are rarely clean — a half-collected dataset,
reviewer comments that contradict each other, a theory section three
drafts deep. The product's provenance discipline is what makes it usable
anyway: it distinguishes what your files actually contain from what it
recalls or infers, tells you which files a synthesis actually drew on,
and treats "this part cannot be settled yet" as a legitimate, recordable
state rather than a gap to fill.

## For experienced researchers

You do not need to rebuild any workflow to get value here. These are
moments, not pipelines: bring the reviewer letter, the design memo, the
outline — as files or pasted text — think it through, take away the
sharpened version, and return to your own tools. If the tool earns a
larger place in your work, [When Concrete Work Is the Main
Task](when-concrete-work-is-the-main-task.md) shows what that looks like.
