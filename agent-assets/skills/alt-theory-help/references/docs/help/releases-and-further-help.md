# Releases and Further Help

## Reading the release line

Alt Theory versions carry their maturity in the name: an `-alpha` tag
means active development — usable, in real research use, and still
moving. The repository's changelog records what each release changed in
plain terms, and the version reference explains the numbering. What
alpha means for compatibility — what is protected (your data,
always) and what may still change (assets, interfaces) — is stated
precisely in [the compatibility policy](../advanced/compatibility-updates-debugging.md).

Before updating, the changelog is the one thing worth skimming; after
updating, reopen a recent conversation and any fallback notices will
tell you if something you relied on moved.

## Getting help, in order of closeness

1. **The Helper, in the app** — for how-do-I and where-is questions,
   guided setup, and plain-language explanation. It answers from current
   documentation and says what it cannot verify.
2. **These docs** — canonical feature pages
   ([System Guide](../README.md#system-guide)), problem-shaped help
   ([Troubleshooting](troubleshooting.md)), boundaries
   ([Known Limitations](compatibility-formats-limitations.md)).
3. **The repository** — `github.com/syuan-research/alt-theory`: source,
   changelog, and the issue tracker.

## Reporting a bug

An alpha product improves at the speed of good reports. A useful one
includes: what you did, what happened, what you expected instead, the
app version and platform, and whether the conversation involved was
imported or had compacted. The
[triage steps](troubleshooting.md#is-it-a-configuration-problem-a-provider-limitation-or-a-bug)
help you file it as the right kind of problem — and "this behaved
dishonestly" (an unlabelled claim, an invented reference, a silent
substitution) is always a bug report here, never a nitpick.

## Feedback and the research program

Alt Theory is also a long-term research effort: what kind of AI support
do scholars actually need, and how would we know? The current product
embodies provisional answers, and disagreement is useful data. If you
want to go beyond using the tool — hard cases where it failed you,
research collaboration, or participating in studies — contact the
project through the repository. Bring difficult examples; that is the
invitation.
